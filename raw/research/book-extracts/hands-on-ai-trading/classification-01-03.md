# Classification of Chapters 1–3

## Scope and labeling convention

This classification uses only Chapters 1–3 of *Hands-On AI Trading with Python, QuantConnect, and AWS*. It is deliberately market-neutral and considers crypto, equities, options, futures, FX, and multi-asset applications. “Textbook claim” means the extract states or proposes the idea. “Classifier inference” means a research implication derived from those statements; it is not presented as a fact established by the book.

The categories overlap. A concept may be foundational knowledge, a transferable method, and an enabling strategic capability at the same time.

## Foundational knowledge

### Markets, prices, and execution

- **Textbook claims:** Executable price depends on side, spread, displayed depth, order type, venue, and timing. Market orders prioritize immediacy but can walk the book; passive limits control price but may not fill. Last sale is not necessarily executable. Fees, spread, slippage, impact, financing, and assignment or liquidation obligations can reverse an apparently profitable result.
- **Applicable markets:** Universal. The US NBBO/SIP/TRF details are equity-specific, while fragmented venue identity is especially important in crypto and also relevant to FX and other multi-venue markets.
- **Classifier inference:** Every alpha, portfolio, and execution study needs an explicit executable-price and cost layer. Cost realism is part of the hypothesis, not a post-processing adjustment.

### Data semantics and time correctness

- **Textbook claims:** Point observations have an event timestamp; period observations are only known after their interval ends. Point-in-time datasets retain late reports, feed errors, and the timing of later corrections. Bars compress data but discard sequence detail. Look-ahead can enter through release dates, fills, universe membership, model vintage, or retrospective idea formation.
- **Applicable markets:** Universal.
- **Classifier inference:** A valid dataset needs both economic/event time and availability time where they differ. Model provenance should include the model's own availability date, not only training-window dates.

### Asset identity and lifecycle

- **Textbook claims:** Ticker strings are not durable identity. Corporate actions, delistings, futures rolls, option expiry/exercise/assignment, settlement rules, multipliers, and venue-specific crypto pairs materially affect accounting and tradability. Continuous futures are analytical representations rather than executable contracts.
- **Applicable markets:** Equities, options, futures, crypto; analogous instrument and venue identity issues apply to FX and multi-asset portfolios.
- **Classifier inference:** Security-master quality and lifecycle-event handling are prerequisites for longitudinal research, not administrative details.

### Strategy and portfolio vocabulary

- **Textbook claims:** Broad styles include momentum, reversion, scalping, and arbitrage. Signals may be continuous or discrete. Capital can be assigned through continuous portfolio allocation, independent bets, or tactical allocation. Strategies often have regime dependence; combining diverse alphas may be preferable to forcing one rule to work everywhere.
- **Classifier inference:** Strategy labels are useful priors, not evidence. A study should define signal, sizing, holding period, exit, benchmark, and regime exposure separately.

### Risk, leverage, and diversification

- **Textbook claims:** Margin creates purchasing power and liquidation risk. Long options generally bound loss at premium, while short options can create much larger assignment obligations. Futures margin changes with conditions. Calm-period correlations may converge during stress. Volatility-adjusted sizing scales quantity inversely with ATR in the chapter's illustration.
- **Classifier inference:** Risk evaluation should include nonlinear payoffs, stress correlations, margin-path dependence, and liquidity, rather than relying on volatility or Sharpe alone.

## Transferable research methods

### Objective-to-hypothesis specification

- **Textbook claim:** Begin with a financial objective, translate it into a measurable target, fix horizon, universe, regulations, operational constraints, risk tolerance, and benchmarks, identify candidate predictors, then state a testable feature-to-target hypothesis.
- **Classifier inference:** Also predefine the action generated by a prediction. Forecast accuracy without a feasible decision rule is not sufficient evidence of trading value.

### Progressive evidence pipeline

- **Textbook claim:** Use cheap exploratory research to reject weak ideas, then event-driven backtesting with frictions and lifecycle events, parameter sensitivity analysis, limited out-of-sample testing, paper trading, and carefully controlled forward deployment. Build and test one layer at a time.
- **Classifier inference:** Each promotion should have a written gate: signal existence, net economic value, robustness, operational feasibility, then forward stability.

### Temporal and universe hygiene

- **Textbook claim:** Deliver period data only when known; respect market calendars and release delays; use point-in-time constituents including delisted assets; avoid selecting today's winners or familiar brands.
- **Classifier inference:** Purged or embargoed temporal splits and event-vintage reconstruction are natural extensions, though those techniques are not named in the extracts.

### Remove, replace, reduce

- **Textbook claim:** Remove non-contributing parameters, replace arbitrary constants with probabilities, causal triggers, direct observations, or adaptive statistics, and reduce repeated exposure to held-out results. Prefer broad parameter plateaus over isolated optima.
- **Classifier inference:** Count implicit parameters—sample dates, universe filters, normalization choices, model vintage, execution assumptions, and benchmark definitions—in the researcher's effective degrees of freedom.

### Hypothesis-driven and data-driven discovery

- **Textbook claim:** A causal hypothesis should be written before testing as a falsifiable cause/effect statement and provides a natural stopping rule when its cause ends. Data-driven anomaly search is legitimate but requires statistical retirement rules because retrospective explanations are easy to manufacture.
- **Classifier inference:** Maintain separate evidence standards: mechanism stability for causal studies and stronger replication/decay monitoring for non-causal discoveries.

### Diagnostic expectations

- **Textbook claim:** Before plotting, state an expected distribution or range so the chart can falsify the expectation. Use limited logs, debugging, charts, and persisted diagnostics to verify timing, state, and order behavior.
- **Classifier inference:** Treat implementation invariants—no pre-availability access, valid indicator state, legal order quantity, conserved accounting—as testable properties.

## Concrete research directions

### 1. Cost-aware short-horizon return or direction prediction

- **Textbook basis:** Chapter 3 proposes next-day equity closing-price prediction using OHLCV, technical indicators, macro variables, and sentiment; Chapters 1–2 require point-in-time inputs and executable fills after costs.
- **Core hypothesis/question:** Do lagged market, macro, and sentiment features predict a future return or direction strongly enough to survive spread, fees, slippage, and turnover?
- **Applicable markets:** Equities, crypto spot/perpetuals, liquid futures, FX; options only with a payoff-aware target rather than raw underlying price.
- **Required data:** Point-in-time OHLCV or quotes, release-timestamped macro data, timestamped news/social sentiment, venue and asset identity, corporate actions or rolls, fees, spreads, and liquidity. For options: chains, expiries, strikes, multipliers, and settlement conventions.
- **Candidate methods/models:** Regularized linear/logistic models, tree ensembles, simple sequence models, calibrated probability models, and cross-sectional ranking. MA, RSI, Bollinger-type transformations, returns, volume, and sentiment are candidate features, not presumed causes.
- **Meaningful baselines:** No-trade; random or majority-direction forecast; previous return/price; simple momentum and mean-reversion rules; buy-and-hold where relevant; linear model on lagged returns alone.
- **Evaluation design:** Predict return or direction rather than unconstrained price level; use chronological walk-forward tests and availability-time-safe features; separate universe construction from model fitting; report calibration and prediction metrics alongside net P&L, turnover, drawdown, and sensitivity to cost assumptions. Reserve a minimally consulted holdout and inspect parameter neighborhoods.
- **Major failure modes:** Nonstationary price-level target, look-ahead in close or release dates, survivorship, sentiment timestamp errors, feature proliferation, a few exceptional trades, cost domination, venue mismatch, and confusing indicator correlation with causation.
- **Continue only if:** The effect repeats across multiple periods and plausible markets or universes, beats simple lagged-price baselines after conservative costs, remains on a broad specification plateau, and is not concentrated in a few assets or events.

### 2. Event and alternative-data signals

- **Textbook basis:** The extracts identify news, regulatory events, weather, shipping, geolocation, transactions, reviews, corporate actions, index additions, drug approvals, fraud allegations, and breaches as possible causes or data sources.
- **Core hypothesis/question:** Does a precisely timestamped event or alternative observation cause a measurable, tradable response beyond ordinary market information?
- **Applicable markets:** Equities and options for corporate/news events; commodity futures for weather/supply events; FX and index futures for macro events; crypto for venue, protocol, regulatory, or flow events; multi-asset for cross-market transmission.
- **Required data:** Original publication/arrival timestamps, revisions, entity/instrument mapping, event attributes, contemporaneous quotes/trades, liquidity, and an unaffected comparison universe. Weather/shipping/geolocation studies need point-in-time source archives.
- **Candidate methods/models:** Event studies, matched controls, difference-in-differences where defensible, survival/hazard models, text classifiers, calibrated event probabilities, and cross-sectional panels.
- **Meaningful baselines:** Market/sector-adjusted return; unconditional event-window return; simple keyword or polarity rule; equal-weight event portfolio; no-event matched samples.
- **Evaluation design:** Predeclare event definition, causal mechanism, reaction and holding windows, and stopping condition. Separate pre-announcement probability signals from post-announcement discrete signals. Test delayed entry, wider spreads, and publication latency; stratify by surprise, liquidity, and market regime.
- **Major failure modes:** Revised or backfilled event data, ambiguous timestamps, entity-resolution errors, crowded/anticipated events, multiple simultaneous causes, selection after observing outcomes, and untradeable opening gaps.
- **Continue only if:** The timing and mechanism survive matched controls, the response is present after realistic latency and costs, magnitude scales sensibly with event surprise, and replication holds in later or independent samples.

### 3. Adaptive cross-asset portfolio rebalancing

- **Textbook basis:** Chapter 3 proposes weekly allocation across equities, bonds, and commodities using returns, volatility, VaR, correlations, macro data, and sentiment to improve risk-adjusted return under diversification and allocation constraints.
- **Core hypothesis/question:** Can state-dependent allocation improve net risk-adjusted outcomes and downside resilience over static diversified portfolios?
- **Applicable markets:** Multi-asset portfolios using equities, bonds, commodities, FX, crypto, and possibly options as overlays.
- **Required data:** Point-in-time total returns, corporate actions, continuous and mapped futures data, FX conversion, margin, financing, spreads, macro vintages, sentiment, and stress-period liquidity/correlation estimates.
- **Candidate methods/models:** Volatility scaling, constrained mean-variance or risk-parity variants, regime classifiers, shrinkage covariance, tactical rules, and regularized predictive allocation models.
- **Meaningful baselines:** Static equal weight; fixed stock/bond mix; buy-and-hold; volatility-scaled static mix; simple trend filter; cash or risk-free benchmark where defined.
- **Evaluation design:** Walk-forward estimation with weekly decisions, explicit turnover and financing costs, fixed allocation/risk bounds, stress subperiods, and comparisons of drawdown, tail loss, turnover, alpha/beta, Sharpe, and probabilistic Sharpe. Test whether gains come from timing, leverage, or simple volatility control.
- **Major failure modes:** Unstable covariance, correlation convergence in crises, macro revisions, futures-roll artifacts, leverage or margin calls, excessive churn, hidden concentration, and optimizing the risk target itself.
- **Continue only if:** Benefits persist after costs and leverage normalization, improve more than one downside/risk metric, survive stress periods and alternative covariance/regime specifications, and outperform simple static and volatility-scaled baselines.

### 4. Liquidity-aware universe selection and factor allocation

- **Textbook basis:** The extracts propose point-in-time fundamental, ETF-constituent, and dollar-volume universes, continuous factor rankings, inverse-volatility weighting, and sensitivity testing of filters.
- **Core hypothesis/question:** Do systematic membership and ranking rules produce stable cross-sectional returns without selection, survivorship, or liquidity bias?
- **Applicable markets:** Primarily equities; adaptable to crypto venues/tokens, futures contract sets, FX crosses, options underlyings, and multi-asset instruments if membership history is reconstructable.
- **Required data:** Point-in-time memberships, delistings, fundamentals with availability dates, prices/volume, total returns, spreads, corporate actions, venue identity, and capacity proxies.
- **Candidate methods/models:** Transparent sorts and ranks, regularized cross-sectional regressions, composite factors, inverse-volatility or constrained equal weighting, and slow-refresh universes.
- **Meaningful baselines:** Broad cap-weighted or equal-weight universe; liquidity-only selection; single-factor rank; ETF/index return; random rank subject to the same liquidity constraint.
- **Evaluation design:** Reconstruct membership at each selection date, lag fundamentals to availability, vary refresh frequency and thresholds, measure turnover/capacity, include delisted assets, and evaluate monotonicity across ranked buckets rather than only a top portfolio.
- **Major failure modes:** Current-constituent leakage, stale fundamentals, arbitrary filter tuning, small/illiquid exposure masquerading as alpha, corporate-action errors, and excessive universe churn.
- **Continue only if:** Rank-return monotonicity and net spread persist across dates and reasonable thresholds, remain after liquidity controls, and are not dependent on a narrow subset of securities.

### 5. Regime-specialized strategy ensemble

- **Textbook basis:** Chapter 2 argues that momentum, reversion, defensive assets, short-biased positions, and news reactions may suit different environments, and that volatility may be more persistent than direction.
- **Core hypothesis/question:** Does conditioning or weighting diverse simple strategies on observable regimes improve robustness relative to each component and to static diversification?
- **Applicable markets:** Equities, futures, FX, crypto, options/volatility, and multi-asset portfolios.
- **Required data:** Long point-in-time histories spanning varied volatility/direction regimes, strategy-level returns, tradable volatility and liquidity measures, costs, and margin requirements.
- **Candidate methods/models:** Rule-based volatility states, hidden-state models, change-point detection, mixture-of-experts, or constrained online weighting. Components should remain simple momentum, reversion, carry/defensive, event, or arbitrage rules.
- **Meaningful baselines:** Best single strategy chosen without hindsight; equal-weight strategy ensemble; static risk-balanced ensemble; simple high/low-volatility switch; underlying buy-and-hold.
- **Evaluation design:** Define regimes only from contemporaneously available data; train and test chronologically; measure switching cost, turnover, drawdowns, component contribution, and performance conditional on regime classification errors. Compare dynamic weights with static combinations.
- **Major failure modes:** Circular regime labels, too few crisis observations, rapid state flipping, correlated alphas during stress, hidden leverage, and allocating to the hindsight-best component.
- **Continue only if:** Dynamic weighting beats static ensembles net of switching costs, gains occur across several regime episodes, results tolerate alternate state definitions, and no single episode explains the improvement.

### 6. Relative-value convergence and fragmented-market arbitrage

- **Textbook basis:** The chapters describe arbitrage across related assets or venues, same-company share-class convergence, independent crypto venue prices, and cross-exchange market making/arbitrage.
- **Core hypothesis/question:** Are price discrepancies between economically linked instruments large and persistent enough to monetize after latency, transfer, inventory, funding, and execution risks?
- **Applicable markets:** Crypto exchanges, dual/share classes and related equities, futures calendar relationships, spot-futures, ETF-underlying relationships, options parity structures, and related FX instruments.
- **Required data:** Synchronized quotes and depth from every leg/venue, fees and rebates, funding/borrow, settlement and transfer latency, instrument identity, contract specifications, and outage history.
- **Candidate methods/models:** Cointegration or error-correction models, robust spread z-scores, parity/residual models, queue-aware execution logic, and inventory-constrained optimization.
- **Meaningful baselines:** Static hedge ratio; no-trade band equal to total costs; naive midpoint convergence; single-venue reversion; simple parity calculation.
- **Evaluation design:** Event-time replay of all legs, conservative simultaneous-fill assumptions, stale-quote rejection, inventory and capital constraints, stress/outage scenarios, and separate gross spread from realized capture.
- **Major failure modes:** Non-synchronous feeds, leg risk, unshortable assets, transfer delays, venue insolvency/outage, changing contract terms, apparent convergence caused by bad ticks, and capacity decay.
- **Continue only if:** Realized capture remains positive with conservative latency and partial-fill assumptions, the relationship has an economic identity/mechanism, and returns survive venue outages and funding/borrow shocks.

### 7. Derivatives volatility-regime positioning

- **Textbook basis:** Chapter 2 illustrates buying a long straddle when high volatility is forecast and selling a short straddle when low volatility is forecast, while Chapters 1–2 emphasize multipliers, expiry, exercise, assignment, margin, settlement, and liquidity.
- **Core hypothesis/question:** Can a volatility forecast select option exposure whose realized payoff exceeds premium, spread, decay, and tail-risk costs?
- **Applicable markets:** Equity and index options; adaptable to options on futures, FX, and crypto where contract rules and data exist.
- **Required data:** Full point-in-time option chains and quotes, underlying prices, expiries, strikes, style and settlement, multipliers, rates/dividends where relevant, exercise/assignment records or models, margin, and transaction costs.
- **Candidate methods/models:** Simple realized-volatility forecasts, regime classifiers, implied-versus-realized comparisons, delta-hedged or unhedged straddles, and constrained spread structures.
- **Meaningful baselines:** Always-long and always-short matched straddles; implied-volatility threshold; historical-volatility forecast; no-trade; underlying volatility exposure if available.
- **Evaluation design:** Use executable bid/ask, exact contract lifecycle, collateral and margin, tail scenarios, multiple expiries/strikes, and risk-normalized comparisons. Separate volatility forecasting skill from option-pricing and execution effects.
- **Major failure modes:** Selling rare-event insurance, assignment/exercise errors, sparse/stale quotes, look-ahead contract selection, volatility risk premium confused with forecast skill, and margin liquidation.
- **Continue only if:** Forecast-conditioned positions improve risk-adjusted and tail-aware results over unconditional option baselines, survive crash scenarios and wider spreads, and are not merely compensated short-volatility exposure.

## Frontier / high-complexity directions

### 8. Reinforcement learning for order execution

- **Textbook basis:** Chapter 3 proposes learning order placement and timing from real-time books, trades, order attributes, news, and historical slippage to improve execution relative to VWAP or TWAP.
- **Core hypothesis/question:** Can a learned sequential policy reduce implementation shortfall or benchmark slippage for a parent order without increasing non-fill, adverse selection, or tail cost?
- **Applicable markets:** Liquid equities/ETFs, futures, FX, crypto, and options with sufficient book depth; venue fragmentation permits routing extensions.
- **Required data:** Event-level depth, trades, queue/order events if available, parent-order metadata, child actions and fills, latency, fees/rebates, cancellations, market calendars, and contemporaneous news/liquidity events.
- **Candidate methods/models:** Contextual bandits, offline RL, constrained actor-critic or value methods, imitation learning from benchmark schedules, and model-based simulators. State, action, reward, partial-fill, and risk constraints must be explicitly defined.
- **Meaningful baselines:** VWAP, TWAP, participation-of-volume, immediate market execution, static limit schedule, and simple adaptive urgency rules.
- **Evaluation design:** Start with offline counterfactual limits acknowledged; use chronological market replay and queue/impact stress models; stratify by order size and liquidity; evaluate implementation shortfall, fill rate, completion risk, variance/tail cost, market impact, and sensitivity to latency. Forward shadow evaluation is needed before causal claims.
- **Major failure modes:** Simulator exploitation, poor support for counterfactual actions, nonstationary market response, reward misspecification, partial-fill accounting, hidden inventory, adverse selection, data leakage from terminal VWAP, and unsafe exploration.
- **Continue only if:** The policy beats strong execution baselines across order-size/liquidity buckets under multiple plausible simulators, retains performance in later data or shadow tests, and improves tail cost without sacrificing completion reliability.

### 9. Informed-flow and adverse-selection detection

- **Textbook basis:** Chapter 1 suggests AI could identify activity patterns associated with informed flow; it also explains that market makers face adverse selection and that speed alone cannot remove genuine informational disadvantage.
- **Core hypothesis/question:** Can public order-flow patterns forecast short-horizon adverse price movement sufficiently to improve quoting, routing, or trade avoidance?
- **Applicable markets:** Equities, futures, FX, crypto, and liquid options wherever quote/trade sequencing is available.
- **Required data:** High-resolution quotes, trades, depth/order events, venue/reporting flags, late-report indicators, subsequent midprice changes, and execution outcomes.
- **Candidate methods/models:** Order-flow imbalance, point processes, sequence models, survival models, anomaly detection, and calibrated toxicity probabilities.
- **Meaningful baselines:** Spread and volatility alone; simple imbalance; recent signed volume; static quote/no-quote policy; random classifier at matched participation.
- **Evaluation design:** Define “informed” operationally as future adverse movement rather than claiming access to private information; use event-time forward labels, latency-safe splits, venue transfer tests, calibration, and an economic test of avoided loss versus missed spread/rebate.
- **Major failure modes:** Unobservable true informed status, circular labels, late prints, trade-sign errors, regime drift, false positives that eliminate profitable participation, and confusing volatility with information toxicity.
- **Continue only if:** Probabilities are calibrated, predict adverse movement beyond spread/volatility/imbalance baselines, transfer across periods or venues, and yield net economic improvement after foregone fills.

### 10. Adaptive multi-alpha allocation with retirement rules

- **Textbook basis:** Chapter 2 proposes many-alpha systems, dynamic weighting, regime specialization, and statistical retirement rules for decaying data-driven alphas.
- **Core hypothesis/question:** Can a bounded allocator identify changing alpha quality quickly enough to improve portfolio outcomes without chasing noise?
- **Applicable markets:** Any multi-strategy, multi-asset setting.
- **Required data:** Point-in-time strategy returns and exposures, costs, capacity, correlations, confidence/PSR estimates, regime variables, and documented strategy birth/version dates.
- **Candidate methods/models:** Shrinkage estimators, Bayesian dynamic models, online learning, constrained bandits, hierarchical risk allocation, decay detection, and explicit quarantine/retirement states.
- **Meaningful baselines:** Equal alpha weights; static risk parity; fixed caps; simple trailing-performance weights; permanent inclusion.
- **Evaluation design:** Simulate strategy discovery dates without backfilling future survivors, include allocator turnover and capacity, test delayed/noisy observations, compare retirement false-positive and false-negative costs, and evaluate crisis correlation convergence.
- **Major failure modes:** Meta-overfitting, survivor-only alpha libraries, feedback loops, rapid weight chasing, correlated failure, understated strategy costs, and endless recycling of holdout data.
- **Continue only if:** Improvements persist with discovery-vintage controls, conservative costs, delayed signals, and alternate retirement thresholds, while drawdown concentration and turnover remain bounded.

## Strategic capabilities

These are not standalone alpha claims; they make research credible across directions.

1. **Point-in-time data and model-vintage layer:** availability timestamps, revisions, late reports, corrections, and model release/training provenance.
2. **Executable-price and cost simulator:** bid/ask fills, depth, partial fills, fees/rebates, slippage, impact, financing, borrow, and latency scenarios.
3. **Cross-asset security master:** durable identity, venue, multiplier, settlement, expiry, exercise, assignment, delivery, corporate actions, delistings, and futures mapping.
4. **Bias-safe universe engine:** historical membership, delisted assets, lagged fundamentals, configurable refresh, and turnover accounting.
5. **Robustness harness:** parameter inventory, broad sensitivity maps, limited holdouts, walk-forward evaluation, influential-trade analysis, and PSR alongside conventional metrics.
6. **Lifecycle-aware derivatives accounting:** option and futures notional, margin, cash/physical settlement, forced-liquidation windows, rolls, and indicator resets.
7. **Research observability:** compact decision logs, expected-range charts, persisted diagnostics, deterministic update sequencing, and warm-up/readiness checks.
8. **Alpha governance:** written causal thesis or anomaly definition, evidence gates, stopping/retirement rules, strategy version dates, and bounded re-evaluation.
9. **Cross-market calendar and scheduling:** market-specific sessions, holidays, shortened days, continuous markets, data releases, and retraining schedules.
10. **Stress and capacity analysis:** stress correlations, spread/depth deterioration, margin changes, assignment, venue outages, and order-size scaling.

The following are useful implementation examples but should not be mistaken for portable market truths:

**Classifier inference:** Portable specifications should describe timestamps, identity, fills, costs, lifecycle, and evaluation behavior independently of any one platform, then map those requirements to platform APIs.

## Claims requiring independent validation

These claims may be educationally useful but are not established for current use merely because they appear in the extracts:

- Time-sensitive counts and coverage: numbers of US exchanges/listed firms/options contracts, data volumes, platform order types, company properties, research examples, live strategies, supported countries, or asset classes.
- Market rules and operational details: odd-lot treatment in NBBO, reporting deadlines, settlement delays, listing thresholds, broker permissions, automatic exercise and FIFO assignment descriptions, tax treatment, margin/leverage limits, and physical-delivery practices.
- Broad empirical claims: opening/closing volume being two to three times midday; volatility being more persistent than direction; moving quickly to tiny live capital being superior; strategy styles matching particular regimes; market makers replenishing at old versus new prices according to inferred information; alternative data or AI revealing informed flow.
- Instrument claims: equity options being generally American/physical with a 100 multiplier, named index options being European/cash-settled with stated multipliers, VIX interpretation, and precise contract availability. These often hold in defined contexts but must be checked contract by contract.
- Risk/statistical claims: any stated interpretation or implementation of PSR without the full estimator; Sharpe benchmark 1.0 as a universal standard; approximate portfolio-margin leverage; return-normality implications; and the illustrative ATR sizing or sentiment “stdev/4” heuristic.
- Example hypotheses: share-class convergence, index-addition buying pressure, weather-to-commodity effects, corporate news reactions, technical features predicting prices, adaptive rebalancing improving Sharpe, or RL improving execution. Each is a proposed test, not a result.
- The chapter's simplified option “notional” formula and early-exercise generalizations require instrument-aware validation.
- Continuous-futures normalization and adjusted-equity histories have no uniquely correct representation; conclusions must be tested across conventions.
