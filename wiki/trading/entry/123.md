# 123 Entry

Purpose: codify Destin's reusable `123` entry lifecycle. This is an entry and sizing primitive, not a complete strategy by itself.

Related context: [[trading/catalog_v1|Trading Catalog]], [[research/trading/ema_px_trend/codification|EMA/PX Trend Continuation Codification]].

## Summary

`123` means:

1. **Breakover / first third** — initial trigger entry.
2. **Pullback / second third** — adverse absorption at a determinate level while the setup remains valid.
3. **Go / final third** — confirmation add only after the position is already in the money or has reconfirmed in-trend.

Short version: **breakover, pullback, go**.

The core risk invariant:

> Do not reach full target exposure while the position is still adverse.

## Textbook Long Example

Observed from the EMA/PX chart-led codification session:

1. Candle closes strongly above the `200 EMA`.
   - Candle Strength Indicator (`CSI`) is around `9`.
   - This is the first third / probe.
2. Candles pull back to the `200 EMA`.
   - The `200 EMA` is the determinate entry level.
   - Pullback quality improves when the pullback candles stay contained inside candle `1`'s open-close body.
   - This is valid adverse absorption, not a failed trade.
3. Another strong candle appears.
   - `CSI` is around `9`.
   - Price makes progress and closes past candle `1` and the pullback candles.
   - If entries occurred at `1` and `2`, the position is now in the money, so the final third can be added.

## General Semantics

### 1. Initial Trigger

The first allocation is a partial entry, commonly one third of intended max size.

It requires a strategy-specific trigger such as:

- strong close over / under a determinate level;
- break of moving average or channel state;
- retest failure followed by trend-direction close;
- high candle-strength confirmation.

### 2. Adverse Absorption

The second allocation is allowed only while adverse movement remains inside the valid setup.

Examples of valid adverse absorption:

- pullback to the `200 EMA` after a bullish breakover;
- countertrend compression toward the `200 EMA` in a bearish regime;
- pullback candles contained inside the trigger candle's body;
- adverse movement toward a planned bid/offer zone without invalidating the trade.

This leg should not be treated as permission to keep averaging into a loser. It is a bounded absorption fill at a determinate level.

### 3. In-Money Confirmation

The final allocation is reserved for confirmation after the trade is already working.

Examples:

- price closes beyond the initial trigger candle and pullback candles;
- position ROE is positive after the first two legs;
- a fresh trend-direction candle confirms progress;
- strategy-specific state reconfirms in-trend behavior.

## Close-Only Backtest Approximation

Manual `123` execution often uses limit orders. Current backtests are candle-close only, so the first automated form must be labeled as an approximation.

Approximation rules:

- enter first third on the close-only trigger;
- allow second third only when price closes into the adverse absorption zone and the setup remains valid;
- allow final third only when unrealized ROE is positive or an explicit confirmation close occurs;
- never allocate the final third solely because price moved farther against the position.

## Open Questions

- Exact CSI threshold for "strong" candles. Current examples use around `9`.
- Whether containment should use candle body only (`open` to `close`) or full candle range in some strategies.
- How strict the determinate level must be: exact touch, close near level, or within a tolerance band.
- Whether default thirds should remain fixed or become continuous by setup quality and volatility.
